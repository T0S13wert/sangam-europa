Philosophy, Art & Culture

Philosophy, literature, art and music, and the long history of scholarly exchange between India and Germany.

Background

  1. Since 1823

    The Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin holds about 10,000 South Asian manuscripts, more than 1,500 of them on palm leaf. Its first acquisitions were two Tamil palm-leaf manuscripts bought by Alexander von Humboldt in Paris in 1823.

    Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin

  2. 1825–1827

    Wilhelm von Humboldt read two papers on the Bhagavad Gītā to the Prussian Academy of Sciences, on 30 June 1825 and 15 June 1826. Hegel answered in a long two-part review, published in 1827.

    Herring, ICPR, Rosenkranz, Life of Hegel

  3. 11–17 July 1930

    Rabindranath Tagore exhibited his paintings in Berlin and visited Albert Einstein at Caputh on 14 July.

    Käthe-Kollwitz-Museum

  4. Today

    The Spitzer manuscript, palm-leaf fragments found on the Silk Road, is the oldest known philosophical manuscript in Sanskrit. It contains the oldest surviving list of the books of the Mahābhārata. Berlin’s Turfan collection of Silk Road manuscripts, about 40,000 fragments, is digitised and online.

    Austrian Academy of Sciences, BBAW

  5. Today

    There are six Goethe-Institutes (Max Mueller Bhavan) in India. The Tagore Centre in Berlin, run by the Indian Council for Cultural Relations, opened in 1994.

    Goethe-Institut, ICCR
